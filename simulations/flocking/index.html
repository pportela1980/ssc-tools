What to notice

Start with all three rules, then turn alignment down to zero. Separation avoids crowding, cohesion pulls neighbours together, and alignment matches headings. Change perception radius to alter who can influence whom. Move your pointer through the group and watch a local disturbance spread. Heading agreement measures direction, not whether the group is doing something useful.

Why it matters

Coordination can emerge from limited local information. No agent sees the whole flock and nobody issues a plan. Different rule balances create different collective motion; a tidy formation is not automatically a better one. This is a minimal Boids-style model, not a model of human leadership.
